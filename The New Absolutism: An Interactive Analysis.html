Deconstructing the Political Compass

Before analyzing ideologies, we must address our language. The terms "left-wing" and "right-wing" are not timeless truths but a historical artifact from the seating arrangement of the French National Assembly in 1789

The Origin of Left vs. Right

During the French Revolution, supporters of the king and the old order (the Ancien Régime) sat to the assembly president's right, while revolutionaries and radicals sat to his left. This accidental seating chart created a political metaphor that has lasted for centuries, often oversimplifying complex political realities.

. Supporters of the King sat to his right; revolutionaries sat to his left.

This simple physical division created a political binary that has polarized populations for over 200 years, simplifying complex beliefs into a one-dimensional conflict.

This analysis will largely avoid these labels. The most important distinction is not left versus right, but frameworks that are open, liberal, and tolerant of dissent versus those that are closed, absolutist, and demand conformity. The danger lies in the structure, not the seat.

The Shared Logic: A World Divided

All absolutist ideologies are built on a simple, powerful premise: history is a struggle between a virtuous in-group and a demonized out-group. This binary is a remarkably effective tool for mobilizing fervor. This same core logic has been adapted and redeployed across the political spectrum, shifting the battleground but keeping the structure of the conflict intact.

Conclusion: Resisting the New Absolutism

The ultimate danger of these ideologies lies in their absolutism. By claiming a total truth that explains all of society's problems, and by dividing the world into a morally pure in-group and a demonized out-group, they shut down the possibility of reason, debate, and progress.
