Deconstructing the Political Compass

Before analyzing ideologies, it's crucial to address the language we use. The terms "left-wing" and "right-wing" are a historical artifact from the seating arrangement of the French National Assembly in 1789

The Origin of Left vs. Right

During the French Revolution (1789-1799), the physical seating arrangement of the National Assembly gave rise to the political spectrum. Supporters of the king, clergy, and the old order sat to the president's right, while anti-monarchist revolutionaries and radicals sat to his left. This accidental geography created a political metaphor that has lasted for centuries, often oversimplifying complex beliefs.

. Supporters of the king sat to his right; revolutionaries sat to his left.

This simple physical division created a political binary that has been used for over 200 years to polarize populations and simplify complex beliefs into a one-dimensional conflict.

This analysis will largely avoid these labels. Instead, it will focus on an ideology's **structure**. The most important distinction is not between left and right, but between frameworks that are **open, liberal, and tolerant of dissent** versus those that are **closed, absolutist, and demand conformity.** The danger lies in the structure, not the seat.

The Shared Logic: A World Divided

All absolutist ideologies are built on a simple, powerful premise: history is a struggle between a virtuous in-group and a demonized out-group. This binary is a remarkably effective tool for mobilizing fervor. This same core logic has been adapted and redeployed across the political spectrum, shifting the battleground but keeping the structure of the conflict intact.

Patriarchy & The "Wage Gap"

Patriarchy is defined as a system where men hold primary power and dominate in roles of political leadership, moral authority, and social privilege. [cite: 9, 548]

An often-cited proof of patriarchy is the "gender pay gap." However, this issue highlights the paradox. The uncontrolled gap (a raw average showing women earn ~83¢ per dollar men earn) is presented as proof of discrimination. But the controlled gap (adjusting for job, experience, hours worked) shrinks to ~99¢.

Focusing on the more divisive "uncontrolled" figure, which ignores individual choices and career paths, while downplaying the near-parity in like-for-like work, is a hallmark of absolutist framing that prioritizes a grievance narrative over nuanced reality.
