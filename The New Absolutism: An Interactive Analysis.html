The New Absolutism

An interactive analysis of how modern ideologies, on both the left and right, endanger open societies.

Our political landscape is fractured. Debates that once revolved around policy and economics have been replaced by intense, often vicious, culture wars. An analysis of the dominant ideologies of our time reveals that this is no accident.

These movements, despite their different goals, share a deep structural DNA that is increasingly at odds with the foundational principles of a tolerant, open society. While the horrors of state Communism seem a world away from a corporate DEI seminar or a nationalist rally, a close examination reveals a shared architecture. This architecture functions like an ideological mind virusA self-replicating system of thought that prioritizes its own propagation over truth and fosters division as a means of control., a system that is essential to understand if we are to diagnose the modern problems of social polarization, the decay of rational discourse, and the rise of a new, decentralized form of absolutism.

The Shared Logic: A World Divided

All absolutist ideologies, whether from the left or right, are built on a simple, powerful premise: history is a struggle between a virtuous in-group and a demonized out-group. This binary framework is a remarkably effective tool for mobilizing fervor. This same core logic has been adapted and redeployed across the political spectrum, shifting the battleground but keeping the structure of the conflict intact.

Wokeness / Critical Social Justice

Core Conflict: Privileged Groups (oppressor) vs. Marginalized Groups (oppressed).

Framework: Creates a complex matrix of interlocking oppressions based on identity. "Privilege" is the new capital in a perpetual, zero-sum conflict over cultural and social power.

Ethno-Nationalism

Core Conflict: The "Pure" Nation (oppressed) vs. Outsiders/Globalists (oppressor).

Framework: Views history as a struggle for ethnic or racial purity. Aims to restore a mythical, homogenous past, often by targeting minorities and immigrants.

Communism

Core Conflict: Bourgeoisie (oppressor) vs. Proletariat (oppressed).

Framework: Views history as a material struggle over the means of production. The utopian goal of a classless society justified the violent overthrow of the state.

Religious Fundamentalism

Core Conflict: True Believers (saved) vs. Infidels/Heretics (damned).

Framework: Aims to govern all of society by divine, inerrant law. Dissent is treated as blasphemy, and truth is subordinate to scripture.

Radical Environmentalism

Core Conflict: The Planet (oppressed) vs. Humanity (oppressor).

Framework: Views humanity as a "virus" on the planet. Aims for a de-industrialized or de-populated world, where the needs of "Gaia" supersede human rights.

The Intellectual DNA

The parallels between these ideologies are not coincidental; they reveal a shared intellectual blueprint. The core logic of Marxism—a binary dialectic of an oppressor class exploiting an oppressed class over resources—has been systematically adapted across the political spectrum.

  • Left-wing ideologies substitute economic class with cultural identity (race, gender, sexuality), retaining the oppressor/oppressed framework.
  • Right-wing ideologies substitute class with national or ethnic identity, framing a struggle against "outsiders" or "decadent" cultural forces.

This shared DNA explains why their rhetorical strategies are so similar, grounded in group-based grievance and aimed at revolutionary or purificatory goals.

The Turn from Fact to Feeling

A crucial evolution has occurred in how these ideologies define truth. Older ideologies, like Communism, rooted their struggle in the material world: the ownership of factories. Modern absolutist movements have taken a profound turn inward, shifting the battleground to the intangible and psychological.

This shift has monumental consequences. When the problem is an invisible system ("systemic racism," "cultural decay") and the primary evidence is a subjective feeling ("lived experience," "national pride"), the entire basis of rational debate changes.

This leads to two powerful ideological tools:

  • Standpoint TheoryThe claim that individuals from a specific group have a more authentic understanding of reality due to their identity or experience.: This creates a "hierarchy of grievance" or "purity," where moral authority is determined by identity, not the validity of an argument.
  • The Weaponization of "Harm" and "Betrayal": Dissenting opinions are no longer merely incorrect; they are framed as acts of "violence," "erasure," or "betrayal," making good-faith debate impossible and justifying censorship.

The New Propaganda: From State Control to Social Contagion

The methods of ideological propagation have also transformed. While Communism relied on a top-down, state-controlled machine, modern movements have perfected a decentralized, networked model of social contagionA phenomenon where ideas, emotions, and behaviors spread rapidly and often unconsciously through social networks, much like a virus..

Top-Down Monolith (Old Model)

  • State-controlled media
  • Systematic indoctrination in schools
  • Pervasive censorship and terror
  • Goal: Total information monopoly

Bottom-Up Network (New Model)

  • Hashtag activism & memetic warfare
  • Institutional capture (HR, NGOs)
  • Cancel CulturePublic shaming campaigns to de-platform or fire individuals who violate ideological norms. & social enforcement
  • Goal: Cultural saturation via peer pressure

Today, social media platforms, with algorithms optimized for engagement, create the perfect environment for this new propaganda. Outrage spreads virally, driven by emotion rather than reason, creating perpetual conflict cycles and extreme social polarization.

The Paradox of Progress

For many of these movements, tangible successes have not led to greater social harmony. As concrete legal goals were achieved, the focus often shifted to more abstract and totalizing goals: dismantling "patriarchy," deconstructing the "gender binary," or purifying the "national soul."

A demand for voting rights is a finite political objective. A demand to dismantle "patriarchy" is an infinite cultural and psychological project that implicates every person's thoughts, language, and behavior. The battleground moves from the statehouse to the soul.

This creates a world of perpetual conflict with no possibility of forgiveness. If the problem is an immutable characteristic like being male, white, or having the "wrong" heritage, and the sin is "privilege" or "impurity," there is no path to redemption, only endless confession and struggle. This framing erodes social cohesion and undermines the principles of universalism and shared humanity that are the bedrock of any functioning, diverse society.

Conclusion: The Dangers of the New Absolutism

The ultimate danger of these ideologies lies in their absolutism. By claiming to possess a total truth that explains all of society's problems and by dividing the world into a morally pure in-group and a demonized out-group, they shut down the possibility of reason, debate, and progress. Communism showed the world the catastrophic consequences of such an ideology when fused with the absolute power of the state.

The critical challenge of the 21st century is to recognize and resist the same ideological architecture wherever it appears, as it operates through the decentralized, contagious, and psychologically potent channels of our modern digital world, before it inflicts irreparable damage on the culture of liberty itself.
