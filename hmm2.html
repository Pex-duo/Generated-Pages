CDM-01: The Great Swipe Divide

The user experience on modern dating apps is profoundly asymmetrical. A stark gender imbalance, with male users outnumbering female users by approximately 3 to 1 on platforms like Tinder [1, 2, 3], creates divergent and often frustrating strategies. This imbalance forces a high-volume, low-selectivity approach for many men, who swipe right on nearly half of all profiles, versus a high-selectivity, high-fatigue approach for women, who swipe right on only 14% of profiles.[2] This dynamic contributes to a widespread feeling of pessimism, with 45% of users reporting frustration with the process.[4]

CDM-02: The Partisan Filter

Increasing political polarization now extends directly into romantic selection, acting as a primary filter.[5] This is particularly pronounced among educated women; nearly three-quarters (73%) of college-educated single women state they would be less likely to date a supporter of Donald Trump.[6] While the aversion is not as strong in the other direction, 39% of single men report being less likely to date a feminist.[6] This ideological sorting mechanism is highly effective, leading to a state of political "homogamy" where 77% of established couples share the same party affiliation, narrowing the potential for connection across the divide.[7]

CDM-03: The Polish Education Paradox

A nuanced reality exists within the Polish gender gap. While women have made significant strides in education, representing a 53% majority of doctoral graduates [8], this academic achievement does not automatically translate to equality in career progression. Poland's score in the "Knowledge" domain of the Gender Equality Index (60.3) lags behind the EU average (64.2).[9] Furthermore, a significant disparity persists at the highest levels of academia, where women hold only 28% of top-tier (Grade A) academic positions, illustrating a persistent glass ceiling despite superior educational attainment.[8]

CDM-04: The Silent Struggle

A significant gender disparity exists in mental healthcare. Women report needing mental health services at a much higher rate than men (50% vs. 35%).[10] However, the path to treatment reveals a complex gap. While women are more likely to seek non-professional support from friends or family (30.9% vs 22.3% of men), they are paradoxically less likely to receive professional care compared to men (20.8% vs 28.1% of men).[11] This suggests that while women more readily identify the need for help, systemic barriers, social stigma, and other factors may prevent them from accessing formal treatment at the same rate as men.[11]

CDM-05: The Kindness Paradox

A global surge in benevolent acts—donating, volunteering, and helping strangers—that began during the pandemic has been sustained, remaining over 10% higher than pre-pandemic levels.[12, 13] Yet, a "pessimism paradox" persists: people consistently underestimate the kindness of their communities. Experiments show lost wallets are returned 1.8 times more often than people expect.[12, 14] This perception matters immensely, as believing in the kindness of others is a more powerful predictor of life satisfaction than a doubling of income.[14]
